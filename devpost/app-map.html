Halo app map

A phone web app for people with face blindness. You add people who agreed, from a few photos and a short note. When the camera is sure it's one of them, an earbud says who they are and how you know them. Everything that touches a face runs and stays in the browser on the phone.

The main pieces. A face reader (face-api models in the browser) turns each face into a 128-number fingerprint. A tracker follows each face over a second or so and gives it one of three states: confident, maybe or no match. A vault keeps people encrypted in the browser's database. A whisper writer turns a note into one line, and say delivers it, following small rules (quiet hours, never-whisper people, the earbud check, buzz codes, event mode's short list): a stored natural voice, the phone's voice, a buzz or a caption. An optional Enhanced mode sends only text to Halo's own Worker, and every request is written to a ledger on the phone.

  1. src/screens/WhisperMode.tsx, anchor const tick = async: each frame goes through detectAll, every face is compared with nearest, and only boxes and distances go on to trackerRef.current.update. The comment "The fingerprints of these faces are dropped here" marks the privacy line.
  2. src/lib/matcher.ts, anchors export const STATE_LIMITS and export class FaceTracker: a face is confident only after at least a second and five frames, one of them facing the camera, under the limit and clearly ahead of the runner-up. If a different face steps into the same spot, the track starts again. Tests: tests/unit/tracker.test.ts.
  3. src/lib/say.ts, anchor export async function sayPerson, with src/lib/rules.ts, anchor export function outputFor: a never-whisper person stays silent, quiet hours and a missing earbud check turn voice into a buzz code, otherwise a stored natural voice or the phone's voice from src/lib/voice.ts.

A practice to reuse

Measure before you trust a threshold, then write down what the numbers don't prove. The spec named one open question: would the match distances separate people? tests/e2e/accuracy.record.ts runs the real face engine on the AI-generated faces and writes eval/accuracy-synthetic.json: same person 0.05 to 0.21, different people 0.70 or more. The limits in STATE_LIMITS (0.42 and 0.50) sit with a margin on both sides, and docs/accuracy.md lists what five synthetic faces can't show (real lighting, bias, relatives). Any project with a cut-off, such as a similarity score, a confidence level or a timeout, can do the same: name the question in the plan, make a script print the real numbers, choose the value with a visible margin, and publish the limits beside it.
