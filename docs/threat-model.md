# Threat model

What could go wrong with Halo, who it would hurt, what the app does about it today, and what is left over. Written for the code in this repository; "residual" means the risk the app does not remove.

Diagrams of where data goes and how consent works: [architecture](diagrams.md#architecture), [privacy data flow](diagrams.md#privacy-data-flow), [recognition flow](diagrams.md#recognition-flow) and [adding someone with their yes](diagrams.md#add-someone-with-their-yes).

## What there is to protect

- **The people a user has added:** names, notes, whisper lines, cue notes, where they met, consent records, 3 to 8 face fingerprints each and one 160 by 160 picture. Stored encrypted in the browser on the user's phone.
- **The fact that the user has face blindness.** Simply having Halo, or a list of people with notes, can reveal it.
- **The people being added.** Their face fingerprints and the notes about them, and their right to say no.
- **Bystanders.** Everyone else the camera sees.

## Threats and mitigations

| # | What could go wrong | What Halo does | Residual |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Misidentification:** Halo names the wrong person, and the user acts on it. | Three states: a name is said only when one face has been steady for a second and five frames, one facing the camera, clearly closer to one person than to anyone else; otherwise "Could be …?" (silent) or "No match". Careful is the default. Children and anyone "held" after wrong matches only ever get "Could be". A look-alike warning when adding someone. Event mode narrows the list. **Wrong person?** notes the mistake on the phone and Settings suggests Careful, holding that person at "Could be", or new photos. The six honest limits are on the landing page, in the app, on the safety page. | Wrong names still happen, more for relatives, look-alikes, children and some groups (see `docs/accuracy.md`). Halo must never be used for trust, safety or access decisions, and says so. |
| 2 | **Missed recognition:** silence for someone the user knows. | "Silence doesn't mean a stranger" is shown in Whisper mode; hints for dim light, distance and angle; Help covers glasses, masks and changed looks; cue notes and Today's list work without the camera. | Silence is common in poor light and at angles. |
| 3 | **Stolen or borrowed phone, unlocked.** Someone opens Halo and reads the list. | Optional lock: a passcode (only a salted PBKDF2-SHA-256 hash is stored) or the phone's own lock through WebAuthn. Auto-lock after idle or time away (not during Whisper mode, so pocket mode keeps working). Five wrong passcodes add a growing wait. **Wipe now** removes everything in two taps. | The lock is a screen, not extra encryption. Someone with the unlocked phone and browser developer tools can reach the browser's storage, where the browser itself can decrypt the vault. Said plainly in the app and on the privacy page. |
| 4 | **Lost or stolen phone, locked.** | The phone's own lock protects the browser. The vault is encrypted with AES-GCM using a key the browser marks non-extractable, so page code can't read it out. There is no account to take over and no server copy. A backup file restores people on a new phone. | A forensic attacker who can run the browser profile could decrypt. Halo can't be wiped remotely (there is no server); the phone's own remote wipe can. |
| 5 | **A malicious helper card or consent link.** Someone sends a link with misleading or hostile content. | Links carry text only, in the URL fragment. The app parses it as JSON, keeps only the expected fields, trims each to a fixed length and shows it as text (no HTML is ever rendered from a link). Nothing is added automatically: the card opens a review, the user checks the note, the person still reads their line and agrees, and photos are taken on the user's phone. A link can't carry faces or fingerprints. | A card can still say something untrue ("this is Raj" about someone else); the in-person yes and photos are the check. |
| 6 | **Shoulder surfing and bystanders hearing names.** | Nothing is said aloud before the earbud check. Quiet hours turn voice into a buzz. Any person can be set to "never whisper". Pocket mode keeps the screen dark. Buzz codes carry no names. | In normal Whisper mode the name is on screen; without earbuds, voice is loud. |
| 7 | **Consent abuse:** adding someone who didn't agree, or faking their yes. | The person being added reads the exact line and ticks "I'm Raj, and I agree" on the phone, or answers by link on their own phone; the method and date are recorded and shown; after a year Halo marks them "Ask again". At most 50 people and 5 new a day. Removal is immediate. The terms make the user responsible. | Halo can't verify who tapped "I agree". |
| 8 | **Use as a surveillance tool:** recognising strangers, tracking where people were seen. | Only enrolled people are compared; unknown faces are dropped after the comparison and never stored or named. No search, no lookup, no location, no history of sightings (the last five whispers live in memory only and are forgotten on close). | A wrong-match report records a person, a time and a distance, on the phone only. |
| 9 | **Data leaving the phone.** | By default nothing about people leaves: the CSP allows `connect-src 'self'`, the build fails if anything loads from another origin, there are no third-party scripts, fonts or analytics, and `tests/unit/privacy-drift.test.ts` fails if the app's network calls or routes drift from the privacy page. Enhanced mode is off by default, text-only, asks first, and lists every request in the ledger. | Dictation, if used, goes through the browser's speech service. The website host sees ordinary request logs. Links sent through a messaging app can be read by that app. |
| 10 | **Abuse of the Enhanced mode server** (cost, denial of service, prompt injection into the cloud writer). | Origin check with no CORS, signed one-hour sessions, strict schemas, size caps, a per-address rate limit and a daily ceiling, no logging of content. The writer's output is only a suggested whisper line, shown as text and editable. | A determined attacker can still use up the daily ceiling, which only turns Enhanced mode off for the day. |
| 11 | **Script injection (XSS).** | React escapes text; no raw HTML is rendered anywhere in the app; the CSP allows scripts only from the site itself, with no inline scripts; link data is parsed, not executed. | Relies on the CSP being served (it is in `_headers`). |
| 12 | **A stolen backup file.** | Locked with AES-GCM and a key derived from the passphrase with PBKDF2-SHA-256 at 250,000 rounds; at least 8 characters. Restoring shows who is inside and adds no one until chosen. | A weak passphrase can be guessed offline. |
| 13 | **Supply chain.** | Dependencies pinned in `package-lock.json`; face models and WebAssembly served from the site, not a CDN. | `@vladmandic/face-api` was archived in February 2025; it gets no fixes. A move to a maintained engine is planned. |
| 14 | **Bias.** | Stated as a limit wherever limits are shown; the accuracy page explains what has and hasn't been tested. | Untested on real people; open face models make more mistakes for darker-skinned and East Asian faces, women, children and older people. |

## Out of scope

- A compromised phone (malware with access to the browser).
- A compromised browser or operating system.
- Physical coercion of the user.
